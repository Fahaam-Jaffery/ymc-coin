# YMC Pre-launch Social & Community Plan

**Status:** Draft strategy · No official social accounts or launch dates supplied.

## Objectives
1. Explain the project direction accurately.
2. Earn credibility with builders through useful technical content.
3. Learn which payment problems and developer needs deserve priority.
4. Establish reliable community moderation and update habits before any token promotion.

## Channel roles
- **X:** Short project notes, technical explainers, ecosystem conversations, progress logs.
- **GitHub:** Canonical source for docs, decisions, issues, and public work.
- **Discord (later, if needed):** Structured builder discussion, contribution channels, office hours.
- **Telegram (optional):** Broadcast announcements and community support only when moderation capacity exists.
- **Instagram (supporting):** Brand explainers, diagrams, short visual stories; not the primary developer channel.

Do not publish account links until the handles are owned and verified. Enable 2FA, assign at least two trusted admins where possible, and publish a clear anti-scam notice.

## Content pillars
1. **Build log (30%)** — decisions, open questions, research summaries, what changed and why.
2. **Payments education (25%)** — payment flows, UX friction, fees, settlement, wallets, user safety.
3. **Builder education (20%)** — L2 concepts, integration patterns, documentation notes; clearly label generic education versus YMC implementation.
4. **Community research (15%)** — polls and interviews about developer pain points; never imply a poll is a product commitment.
5. **Brand and mission (10%)** — identity, values, team updates when verified, visual storytelling.

Percentages are a starting recommendation and should be adjusted based on useful engagement, not vanity metrics.

## Sustainable cadence
- 2–3 X posts per week.
- 1 substantial GitHub update or short research note every 1–2 weeks.
- 1 monthly community Q&A or builder feedback session once a moderator and clear agenda exist.
- Repurpose a strong research note into one diagram and one short social thread; do not manufacture daily hype.

## First four weeks
### Week 1 — Introduce the problem
- Post: “We’re exploring payments infrastructure for builders. Here’s what we’re trying to learn.”
- Publish the project status and open questions in GitHub.
- Ask developers what causes the most friction in cross-app or on-chain payment flows.

### Week 2 — Teach, don’t promise
- Explain what Layer 2 means, including trade-offs and trust assumptions.
- Share a glossary of L1, L2, settlement, sequencer, bridge, and finality.
- State clearly that YMC's specific architecture is not selected.

### Week 3 — Share the design process
- Publish a decision matrix for evaluating candidate chains and L2 stacks.
- Share brand identity and request feedback on clarity/accessibility.
- Invite technical feedback without promising grants, token rewards, or partnership status.

### Week 4 — Report back
- Summarize community feedback and explain what did or did not change.
- Publish the next set of research questions.
- Review metrics and refine the content mix.

## Example copy
**Project introduction**  
“We're exploring a payments-focused Layer 2 project for builders. The chain, architecture, and token design are still under evaluation. We're sharing the questions early because useful infrastructure starts with clear assumptions.”

**Builder research prompt**  
“What's the hardest part of building a good on-chain payment experience today: onboarding, fees, confirmation UX, asset movement, or something else? We're collecting problems to investigate—not announcing features.”

**Progress note**  
“This week: mapping the trade-offs behind L2 architecture choices. No chain has been selected yet. We'll publish the decision criteria before making a recommendation.”

## KPIs
- Qualified developer replies and conversations.
- GitHub visits, stars, issues, and useful technical contributions.
- Documentation engagement and completion.
- Community questions answered within the stated support window.
- Feedback that changes product priorities.
- Reported scam attempts and moderation response time.

Follower count and impressions are context, not the sole success metric.

## Safety and credibility rules
- No price predictions, guaranteed returns, artificial urgency, or implied investment upside.
- No fabricated partnerships, team credentials, audits, exchange listings, throughput, fee claims, or launch dates.
- No promised airdrops or staking rewards until formally designed, reviewed, and approved.
- Label mockups and concept diagrams as concepts.
- Never ask users to share seed phrases, private keys, or pay an “activation fee.”
- Pin official links and anti-impersonation guidance when official accounts exist.
- Obtain legal review for token marketing and promotions in target jurisdictions.
